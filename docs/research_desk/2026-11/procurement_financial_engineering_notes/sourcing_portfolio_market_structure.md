Legend: [EMP] = empirical result; [THEO] = theoretical/model result; [NUM] = numerical/simulation study; [FOUND] = pre-2015 foundational work; [UNVERIFIED-SESSION] = citation known from prior literature knowledge but NOT re-verified by a web fetch in this research session (author/title believed correct; check DOI before quoting).

## (1) Mean-variance / CVaR / robust optimization for multi-supplier or multi-origin commodity procurement

### Takeaway
There is a well-developed OR literature on "procurement portfolios" (long-term contracts + options + spot, across multiple suppliers) with CVaR/risk-averse objectives (Omega, EJOR, Annals of OR, JORS), and a smaller ag-econ literature applying Markowitz mean-variance directly to *national import origin* allocation (Karakoc & Konar 2024 on grain imports). Core result across both: diversification across origins/channels can cut cost volatility at little or no added mean cost, but supply-capacity constraints of exporters bind and some importers face a real cost-risk trade-off.

### Cited Findings
- [EMP/NUM] Karakoc & Konar (Environmental Research: Food Systems, 2024), "Optimization of national grain imports to balance risk and return: a portfolio theory approach," DOI 10.1088/2976-601X/ad22d1. Apply Markowitz mean-variance to national grain import "accounts," treating each importer as a portfolio manager and each exporter as an asset, under realistic supply-mass (export capacity) constraints; COMTRADE data 2008–2020. Find risk can be reduced *without raising cost* for Egypt wheat, Vietnam maize, and Saudi Arabia rice imports, whereas for Turkey wheat a more stable supply mix would cost more. Distinguishes "optimal" portfolios (min price volatility subject to cost constraint) vs "optimum" (min volatility unconstrained). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/2976-601X/ad22d1); [PDF](https://wastedfood.american.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/OptimizationOfNationalGrainImportsToBalanceRiskAndReturnAPortfolioTheoryApproach.pdf); [Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/optimization-of-national-grain-imports-to-balance-risk-and-return/)
- [EMP] A Mexican wheat-import mean-variance origin model (Journal of Agricultural and Applied Economics / AgEcon jaae232) reports out-of-sample forecasts showing the mean-variance model gives an accurate depiction of actual Mexican import shares — i.e., importers' observed origin shares are consistent with risk-aversion over landed price. (Exact authors/year not confirmed in session.) — [AgEcon PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/56646/files/jaae232.pdf)
- [NUM] Wilson & Dahl, "Procurement Strategies to Improve Quality Consistency in Wheat Shipments," Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics (2008); earlier NDSU AAE Report 595 (2007). Simulation + stochastic dominance comparing importer purchase strategies; computes joint probabilities of meeting quality specs and costs for alternative strategies (quality/origin-spec risk rather than price-portfolio). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ags/nddaae/23498.html); [PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/23498/files/aer595.pdf); [DOAJ JARE 2008](https://doaj.org/article/86e2b647068d49c7bf68e6444ff46564)
- [THEO/NUM] Merzifonluoglu (Omega, 2015): parts procured via forward contracts, option contracts or spot purchases, with risk-neutral and risk-averse (CVaR) objectives — a standard template for mixing term contracts (e.g., annual soy-oil supply contracts with crushers) with spot cargoes. (Title/DOI not retrieved in session.) — [search snippet via Tilburg repository listing](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/risk-averse-supply-portfolio-selection-with-supply-demand-and-spo/)
- [THEO/NUM] "Risk averse supply portfolio selection with supply, demand and spot market volatility" (Tilburg University authors; Omega) — supplier selection + spot market under supply, demand and spot-price volatility with risk aversion. — [Tilburg](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/risk-averse-supply-portfolio-selection-with-supply-demand-and-spo/)
- [THEO/NUM] "Integrated demand and procurement portfolio management with spot market volatility and option contracts" (EJOR, 2017): selects a supply portfolio instead of a single procurement mode; normally distributed random variables with an exact solution method. — [Tilburg](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/integrated-demand-and-procurement-portfolio-management-with-spot-/)
- [THEO/NUM] Mohammadivojdan, Merzifonluoglu & Geunes (EJOR 297(3):917–929, 2022), "Procurement portfolio planning for a newsvendor with supplier delivery uncertainty": allocate quantities of an input across multiple unreliable suppliers. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:297:y:2022:i:3:p:917-929); [Tilburg repo](https://repository.tilburguniversity.edu/items/2038790d-b3bc-4baa-aff3-11dde77dcc29)
- [THEO/NUM] Shi et al. (JORS, 2011), "A portfolio approach to managing procurement risk using multi-stage stochastic programming": CVaR to measure procurement risk across long-term contracts and spot purchases. — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/a-portfolio-approach-to-managing-procurement-risk-using-multi-stage-stochastic-programming-shi/10009320574)
- [NUM] IJPE vol. 167 (2015) pp. 128–138 — procurement risk paper surfaced in the same search cluster (topic: risk-averse procurement; title not confirmed in session). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/proeco/v167y2015icp128-138.html)
- [FOUND][UNVERIFIED-SESSION] Martínez-de-Albéniz & Simchi-Levi (2005), "A portfolio approach to procurement contracts," Production and Operations Management 14(1):90–114 — classic result: optimal portfolio of long-term, option and spot contracts has a threshold/merit-order structure (contracts ranked by fixed vs. variable price).
- [FOUND][UNVERIFIED-SESSION] Haksöz & Seshadri (2007), "Supply chain operations in the presence of a spot market: a review with discussion," Journal of the Operational Research Society 58:1412–1429 — survey of contract + spot procurement.
- [THEO][UNVERIFIED-SESSION] Kouvelis, Li & Ding (2013), "Managing storable commodity risks: the role of inventory and financial hedge," M&SOM 15(3):507–521 — joint inventory + futures hedging for storable commodities (relevant to tank-storage + CBOT soybean oil hedge decisions).

### Inferences
- For a Korean soybean-oil importer, the Karakoc–Konar framework maps directly: assets = origins (AR, BR, US, plus VN/CN as re-export/crush origins), returns = landed cost per tonne (negative), covariance from landed-cost history, capacity constraints = exportable surplus/shipment sizes per origin. Because AR/BR/US FOB soy-oil prices share the CBOT soybean-oil futures component, covariance is dominated by the common futures leg; diversification benefit therefore comes almost entirely from the *basis + freight + FX* legs, which argues for running the portfolio on decomposed landed-cost components (see Q5).
- The OR "procurement portfolio" literature suggests modeling the decision as term contract (with a crusher/trader) + options/flex + spot cargo per origin with a CVaR constraint, rather than pure mean-variance, since landed-cost distributions are skewed by logistics shocks (Paraná low water, Gulf hurricanes).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve full numerical results (e.g., % CVaR reduction) for the Omega/EJOR/Annals papers (paywalled/403).
- No paper found specifically on *vegetable-oil* multi-origin import portfolio optimization; nor any on Korean importers.
- Robust (distributionally robust / uncertainty-set) multi-origin commodity procurement: no specific peer-reviewed paper verified in this session.

## (2) Black-Litterman / Bayesian view-blending outside equities (commodities, procurement, supply chains)

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed application of Black-Litterman (BL) to commodity *procurement* or origin allocation was found. The closest is Shi & Irwin's empirical-Bayesian (BL-tradition) futures hedging framework for ag commodities, and the general BL ↔ Bayesian-regression literature (Kolm & Ritter) that shows how views can be generated by forecasting models with explicit uncertainty — which is the natural bridge to a procurement setting.

### Cited Findings
- [THEO/EMP] Shi & Irwin (2004/2005 conference papers; NCCC-134 / AgEcon sp04-26, sp05sh06), "Optimal hedging with a subjective view: an empirical Bayesian approach" — blends a hedger's subjective views with market-consensus parameters to set futures hedge ratios, explicitly in the Black-Litterman tradition. [FOUND] — [AgEcon sp04-26](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/11810/files/sp04-26.pdf); [AgEcon sp05sh06](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/19155/files/sp05sh06.pdf)
- [THEO] Kolm & Ritter (2017, "On the Bayesian interpretation of Black-Litterman," EJOR) — show the duality between BL optimization and Bayesian regression; views are treated as noisy observations; the most general BL-type model is presented. Views can be on portfolios (linear combinations of assets), not just single assets. — [PDF](https://cims.nyu.edu/~ritter/kolm2017bayesian.pdf)
- [THEO] Kolm, Ritter & Simonian (2021), "Black-Litterman and beyond: the Bayesian paradigm in investment management" (Journal of Portfolio Management) — survey emphasizing choice of priors, view generation, transaction costs and factor models. — [PDF](https://cims.nyu.edu/~ritter/kolm2021black.pdf)
- [THEO] Spears, Zohren & Roberts (arXiv 2301.13594, 2023) — extend BL to multiple views on the same asset, with view parameters estimated from forecasting models plus their uncertainty estimates (i.e., views = model forecasts, Ω = forecast error variance). — [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.13594)
- [THEO] "Black-Litterman, Bayesian Shrinkage, and Factor Models in Portfolio Selection: You Can Have It All" (arXiv 2308.09264) — unifies BL with shrinkage and factor models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09264)
- [NUM] "LLM-Enhanced Black-Litterman Portfolio Optimization" (arXiv 2504.14345, 2025) — views generated by LLMs; equity context. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14345)
- [FOUND][UNVERIFIED-SESSION] Black & Litterman (1992), "Global portfolio optimization," Financial Analysts Journal 48(5):28–43.

### Inferences
- A procurement analogue: prior = "equilibrium" landed-cost spreads implied by forward curves (CBOT soybean-oil futures + quoted FOB premiums/basis curves + FFA freight + FX forwards); views = analyst/model forecasts of basis or freight for specific origins (e.g., "Argentine Up River basis will weaken vs Paranaguá by $X/t"), with confidence Ω from the model's historical forecast-error variance (per Spears et al.). Posterior expected landed costs then feed the Q1 mean-variance/CVaR allocation. This is an inference — no published procurement application found.
- Views on *spreads between origins* are naturally "portfolio views" (long one origin, short another) in Kolm–Ritter's sense.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed BL application to commodity procurement, supplier allocation or supply chains found in this session (searches returned only practitioner procurement-risk content and equity BL).
- No BL application to commodity futures portfolios was verified (only hedging via Shi & Irwin).

## (3) Value of information / forecast value in procurement — quantified results

### Takeaway
Theory and numerical studies consistently find forecast/information value is positive but diminishing in accuracy, and larger for more risk-averse decision makers; in the presence of liquid futures, price forecasts have value only for strongly risk-averse firms or when they beat the futures market. Specific quantified percentages could not be extracted from the fetched abstracts.

### Cited Findings
- [NUM] "Procurement portfolio management for resilient supply chains: Value of information for risk averse decision making" (Tilburg University authors; Annals of Operations Research, DOI 10.1007/s10479-024-05918-5): multi-stage stochastic programs with demand and supplier-disruption updates under risk-neutral and CVaR objectives; selects procurement portfolios conditional on availability and quality of updated information, compares risk-averse vs risk-neutral strategies under varying information updates. — [Tilburg](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/procurement-portfolio-management-for-resilient-supply-chains-valu/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-024-05918-5)
- [THEO/NUM] "The value of commodity price forecasts in the presence of futures under uncertainty" (UT Austin thesis): a commodity producer/consumer must be *highly* risk averse for price forecasts to have value when considering profit maximization alone (because futures already allow locking prices); positive and diminishing marginal returns to forecast value from accuracy improvements in most cases. — [UT repository](https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/handle/2152/46597?show=full)
- [THEO][FOUND] AAEA 1989 paper (AgEcon 270477): value of information increases with risk aversion. — [AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/270477); [PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/270477/files/aaea-1989-014.pdf)
- [THEO/NUM] "Commodity Procurement with Demand Forecast and Forward Price Updates" — forward contracts + spot; isolates the value of demand-forecast updates vs forward-price updates, separately and jointly, on procurement policy value. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/85402588)

### Inferences
- For an importer with access to CBOT soybean-oil futures, the *futures leg* forecast is likely low-value (market-efficient, hedgeable); the decision-relevant information is in non-hedgeable legs — origin basis/FOB premium, freight, FX — consistent with the UT thesis logic. Forecasting effort should be focused there.

### Gaps
- No quantified % cost/CVaR improvement retrieved (full texts paywalled). Report-writer should not cite specific magnitudes from these papers.
- No study found quantifying forecast value specifically for vegetable-oil import timing/origin choice.

## (4) Market power / oligopoly in grain and oilseed trade (ABCD + COFCO)

### Takeaway
The most rigorous recent evidence — Wilson, Bullock & Dubovoy (AEPP 2025), using vessel-nomination (shipment-level) data — finds concentration in international grain/oilseed trading is *much lower than traditionally suggested*, with a viable competitive fringe of new entrants and "fierce" competition. Claims of high market power (ABCD controlling 70–90% of trade, profit tripling) come mostly from NGO/advocacy reports using descriptive data. No peer-reviewed study verified in this session shows trader concentration moving *futures* prices; economically, any market-power effect would show up in basis/FOB premiums, origination spreads and crush margins rather than in the CBOT price.

### Cited Findings
- [EMP] **VERIFIED:** Wilson, W.W., Bullock, D.W. & Dubovoy, I. (2025), "Dynamic changes in the structure and concentration of the international grain and oilseed trading industry," *Applied Economic Perspectives and Policy* 47(4):1561–1582 (Sept 2025), DOI 10.1002/aepp.13524. Uses detailed transaction data derived from vessel nominations to measure concentration across geographies and commodities and to identify firm clusters; finds "levels of concentration are much less than traditionally suggested," many new entrants form a viable competitive fringe, three clusters of firms, competition characterized as intense. Working paper: NDSU AAE Report 830 (May 2025). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/apecpp/v47y2025i4p1561-1582.html); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:apecpp:v:47:y:2025:i:4:p:1561-1582); [NDSU AAE830 PDF](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/358647/files/AAE830.pdf); [AAE830 supplement](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/358648/files/AAE830-S.pdf)
- [EMP, secondary] Press coverage of the same AEPP study says the competitive fringe includes newer players such as Russian and Chinese firms. — [farms.com](https://www.farms.com/news/global-grain-trade-gets-a-shake-up-what-it-means-for-canadian-farmers-231743.aspx)
- [Advocacy/descriptive] SOMO, "Hungry for profits": the five largest traders' profits tripled in the recent three years vs prior years; attributes part to profiteering / "seller's inflation"; asserts the five firms control 70–90% of global commercial grain trade (industry-share estimate, not econometric markup). — [SOMO](https://www.somo.nl/hungry-for-profits/)
- [Policy report, method unclear] HSE BRICS Competition Law and Policy Centre (2025), "From Farm to Futures": estimates BRICS grain producers lose ~$2.5bn/year; discusses trader oligopoly, financialisation, information asymmetry, co-opetition. Methodology not visible in coverage — treat cautiously. — [Agrolatam](https://www.agrolatam.com/news/brics-grain-losses-trader-oligopoly-digital-platforms-study/); [APAnews](https://apanews.net/brics-grain-producers-lose-2-5b-yearly-to-manipulation-study/)
- [EMP] Country-level concentration: US, Brazil, Argentina = 81.6% of world soybean production and 97.2% of exports (2018/19); China-import-side study argues high concentration alone does not establish monopoly power — seasonality/regionality of production shifts power between seasonal suppliers. — [Economic Research–Ekonomska Istraživanja 2022/23](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/1331677X.2022.2142818); [Hrčak](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/442844)
- [EMP][FOUND] Older AgEcon paper tests imperfect competition in world soymeal exports: >95% of exports from Argentina, EU, US, Brazil; Argentine/Brazilian firms advantaged by lower costs and export-promotion policies (differential export taxes). — [AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/31010/files/22010078.pdf)
- [EMP] Bekkerman & Taylor (NCCC-134, 2016), "Market concentration in the wheat merchandizing industry": elevator concentration and spatial competition affect prices offered to farmers; effect of market power on farm prices likely varies by commodity (i.e., market power shows up in local basis). — [PDF](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_2016/pdf/Bekkerman_Taylor_NCCC-134_2016.pdf)
- [EMP] Dhingra & Tenreyro (LSE working paper), agribusiness middlemen/buyer power: two-thirds of world crop price increases were received by the exporting country as aggregate export gains (low-income countries, 2000s) — evidence on intermediary capture of price changes. — [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/tenreyro/agri.pdf)
- [EMP] NCCC-134 (2026) paper: Brazilian real and global benchmark soybean price jointly determined given Brazil's export share — origin FX transmits into the world price. — [farmdoc PDF](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_2026/pdf/Leonard_Twizeyimana_Twizeyimana_Avileis_NCCC-134_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a soybean-oil importer, trader structure matters mainly through FOB premiums/basis at Up River (Rosario), Paranaguá and US Gulf, and through crush-margin capture — not through CBOT. Wilson et al. (2025) suggest the selling side is more contestable than the ABCD narrative implies, which supports tendering across a broad seller list (including fringe/Asian traders) rather than assuming oligopoly pricing.
- Note the Wilson et al. study is on grains/oilseeds shipments; whether soybean *oil* (shipped by crushers, partly in parcel tankers) shows the same low concentration was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Exact HHI/CR4 numbers and commodity-by-origin breakdown from Wilson et al. (2025) could not be retrieved (403 on PDF). Report-writer should cite qualitative findings only.
- No peer-reviewed econometric study found estimating ABCD/COFCO markups or testing whether trader concentration affects futures prices vs. basis. This is a genuine gap in what was found, not necessarily in the literature.

## (5) Landed-cost / CIF decomposition and basis forecasting methods for importers

### Takeaway
Soybean oil export prices are quoted as CBOT soybean-oil futures + FOB basis/premium (in points or $/t) per origin; landed (CIF/CFR) cost adds ocean freight, insurance, FX and import duties. Origin basis is driven by farmer selling pace, crush rates/margins, biodiesel demand and logistics (Paraná draft, Gulf disruptions). For forecasting basis, the ag-econ literature shows simple historical averages (optimal window crop-specific) are hard to beat, current-market deviation helps at some horizons, and nonlinear/composite models can improve further.

### Cited Findings
- [EMP, market data] Decomposition in practice: S&P Global Platts (16 Sept 2026) — Argentine Up River soy oil for October loading assessed at $1,223.13/mt on 15 Sept; October Up River basis −1,440 points vs CBOT October; Brazil Paranaguá basis −1,420; Brazil premium over Argentina narrowed to ~$4.40/mt. — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/091626-argentina-soybean-oil-gains-as-brazil-premium-narrows-amid-fresh-supply-data); [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1148561)
- [EMP, market data] Basis regimes shift: late 2022 Brazil FOB soy-oil/meal basis fell below Argentina's (weak Argentine farmer selling, low crush) — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/102622-brazil-soybean-meal-oil-premiums-drop-below-argentina); Aug 2023 South American soy-oil FOB basis hit all-time lows since Platts began assessments — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/081823-south-american-soybean-oil-fob-basis-drops-to-all-time-lows); Sept 2022 Argentine soy-oil premiums above Brazil on supply concerns — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/090122-supply-concerns-send-argentina-soy-oil-premiums-above-brazil); Argentine premiums surpassed US bases with Indian import-duty changes cited as a demand driver — [Fastmarkets](https://fastmarkets.com/?p=68688)

### Inferences
- Recommended decomposition for a Korean importer per origin o: Landed_KRW/t(o) = [CBOT BO futures (¢/lb → $/t) + FOB basis_o] + Freight_o→Korea + Insurance + other port costs, × USD/KRW, × (1 + tariff_o) (+ TRQ/FTA effects). Only the futures leg is common across AR/BR/US; basis, freight and tariffs (Korea's FTA status differs by origin, e.g., KORUS for US) differentiate origins. VN/CN supply is typically re-export or crushing of imported beans, so their price will track AR/BR/US basis plus regional crush margin. (Inference; Korean tariff schedule not verified in this session.)
- Basis forecasting baseline: historical-average (multi-year, same delivery month) seasonal basis + current-deviation adjustment, benchmarked against nonlinear/ML models; drivers to include: farmer selling pace/FX (AR export-tax regime, BRL), crush margins, biodiesel mandates, river draft/port weather.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found on forecasting *South American FOB soybean-oil premiums* specifically, or on importer-side landed-cost models for vegetable oil.
- Korean soybean-oil import tariff/TRQ by origin not verified.
- Basis forecasting evidence above is US interior grain basis; transferability to export FOB premiums is an inference.
