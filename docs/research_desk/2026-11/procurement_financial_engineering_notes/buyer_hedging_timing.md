# Hedging and Purchase-Timing Models for Commodity Buyers (Soybean Oil / Palm Oil / Soy Complex)

Scope note: research was done on 2026-11-19 with web search plus abstract and landing-page fetches. ScienceDirect full texts returned HTTP 403, and two open PDFs came back as binary, so most numbers below come from abstracts, repository pages, or search-engine summaries of the papers. Each item says which. "OOS" means out-of-sample and "IS" means in-sample. Sources marked "[not verified this session]" come from background knowledge only. They are listed as pointers to check, not as findings.

## Q1. Minimum-variance, CVaR/ES and utility-based hedge ratios: which estimation methods improve OOS hedging effectiveness for soybean oil, palm oil and soybeans, and by how much?

### Takeaway
For agricultural and veg-oil futures, time-varying hedge ratios (GARCH/DCC, regime-switching, copula) usually beat a constant OLS minimum-variance ratio in-sample. Out-of-sample the gain is small, mixed, or negative, because the noise in a time-varying ratio cancels out much of its theoretical advantage. A buyer should treat rolling-window OLS as the baseline that any fancier model has to beat on OOS variance or ES reduction net of rebalancing costs. Veg-oil-specific OOS evidence is thin. Palm oil (FCPO) studies report low hedge effectiveness and hedge ratios well below 1.

### Cited Findings
- **Soybean oil, nonparametric vs BGARCH (OOS):** Fan et al. (2016), "Estimation and Hedging Effectiveness of Time-Varying Hedge Ratio: Nonparametric Approaches," *Journal of Futures Markets* 36(10):968–991, DOI 10.1002/fut.21766. The paper tests soybean oil, corn, S&P 500 and Hang Seng futures. Its nonparametric time-varying hedge ratio is supported both IS and OOS. It also reports that when the estimated time-varying ratio is highly variable (as BGARCH ratios often are), it hedges worse than the unconditional constant ratio. Source: abstract via search summary. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/fut.21766); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v36y2016i10p968-991.html)
- **Soybean oil, higher-moment hedging (OOS):** Cerny et al., "Optimal Hedging With Higher Moments" (Bayes Business School working paper; year and journal version not verified). Soybean oil is among the commodities where using OLS (variance-only) hedging when returns are non-normal causes a systematic wealth loss. Its finding is that "at best, MGARCH models have led to very modest improvements in gross hedging efficiency" OOS. Source: search summary of the PDF. — [PDF](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/368385/ales-cerny-optimal-hedging-with-higher-moments.pdf)
- **Dalian soybean oil, bivariate GARCH / GJR-GARCH (OOS):** A Tamkang University (Taiwan) thesis estimates dynamic hedge ratios for DCE soybean oil futures and evaluates OOS hedging performance. Numbers were not retrievable, and this is not peer reviewed. — [TKU ETDS](https://etds.lib.tku.edu.tw/ETDS/Home/Detail/U0002-0209201114443900)
- **Soybeans, EC-GARCH vs OLS cross-hedge:** A 2024 paper, "Comparative analysis of futures contract cross-hedging effectiveness for soybean: models and insights" (Taylor & Francis, DOI 10.1080/03031853.2024.2401787; the ISSN in the DOI is *Agrekon*'s, so this is likely the South African market, but not verified). It reports an EC-GARCH hedge ratio of about 0.80 with hedge effectiveness of about 0.47, roughly the same as OLS. The authors say EC-GARCH "controls risk as efficiently as OLS" despite the slightly lower ratio. Whether this is IS or OOS was not confirmed. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03031853.2024.2401787)
- **Corn and soybeans, DCC-GARCH:** Park & Jei (2010) is cited in secondary sources as finding that DCC-GARCH gives only small variance-reduction gains over OLS for corn and soybeans. This is secondary citation only; I could not retrieve the original. — via search summary of [MDPI JRFM 2024 wild-bootstrap hedge ratio paper](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/17/7/310)
- **Soybean complex, multiproduct and regime-switching (OOS):** An Indian agricultural-futures study using multiproduct time-varying hedge ratios reports a "substantial reduction in margin variance" over single-product time-varying and naive hedges, both IS and OOS. OOS ratios were about 0.82 for soybean and 0.74 for soybean meal. A related regime-switching dynamic-correlation (RSDC) study reports IS margin-variance reduction more than double that of a naive 1:1 hedge, narrowing to about 41% improvement over naive OOS. Which commodity the 41% figure belongs to is not clear from the snippet. — [AgEcon Search: Multiproduct Optimal Hedging by Time-Varying Correlations in a State-Dependent Regime-Switching model](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/61679/)
- **Regime-switching with a fundamental driver:** A soybean-complex study uses the soybean stocks-to-use ratio as the variable that drives the probability of switching between correlation regimes. No OOS number was retrievable. — same AgEcon Search record as above
- **Regime-switching, general OOS record:** In corn and nickel, Markov regime-switching (MRS) correlation-GARCH hedges show IS gains but only "marginal dominance" OOS over no-hedge and constant OLS. Another study says MRS ratios win IS but "results from the out-of-sample analysis are mixed." Some papers, including a four-regime jump-switching model, do report OOS superiority. — [Optimal hedging with a regime-switching time-varying correlation GARCH model (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229702856_Optimal_hedging_with_a_regime-switching_time-varying_correlation_GARCH_Model); [Optimal futures hedging under jump switching dynamics (J. Empirical Finance, ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092753980800100X); [Bivariate MRS-GARCH MVHR (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24075920_A_bivariate_Markov_regime_switching_GARCH_approach_to_estimate_time_varying_minimum_variance_hedge_ratio)
- **Palm oil (FCPO, Bursa Malaysia), OLS MVHR:** Using 2009–2011 data, OLS hedge ratios ranged from about 36% to 67% across contract months, and the paper concludes that hedging effectiveness of the FCPO market is low. — [Ong & Tan, "Hedging Effectiveness of Crude Palm Oil Futures Market in Malaysia" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267781069_Hedging_Effectiveness_of_Crude_Palm_Oil_Futures_Market_in_Malaysia); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Hedging-Effectiveness-of-Crude-Palm-Oil-Futures-in-Ong-Tan/9f834176899c355a0d1b0981a88e0b4728bcfe58)
- **Palm oil, extended mean-Gini (downside-risk-aware) hedge ratio:** The optimal ratio is 57.62% of CPO exposure. — ["The Hedging Effectiveness of Malaysian Crude Palm Oil Futures: An Application of the Extended Mean-Gini Model" (SCIRP)](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=86797)
- **Palm oil, energy cross-hedge:** Among non-palm energy futures, Tokyo crude oil futures were the best alternative cross-hedge for physical CPO. — ["Effective cross hedging: Evidence from physical crude palm oil and its non-interrelated energy futures contracts" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333842962_Effective_cross_hedging_Evidence_from_physical_crude_palm_oil_and_its_non-interrelated_energy_futures_contracts)
- **Expected-shortfall evaluation, Italian field crops:** The benchmark is a constant OLS MVHR, with risk evaluated by ES. For soybeans, hedging about 72% of production value cut financial risk by about 46%. This is the producer side; IS vs OOS was not confirmed. — ["Hedging Effectiveness of Commodity Futures Contracts to Minimize Price Risk: Empirical Evidence from the Italian Field Crop Sector" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356693635_Hedging_Effectiveness_of_Commodity_Futures_Contracts_to_Minimize_Price_Risk_Empirical_Evidence_from_the_Italian_Field_Crop_Sector)
- **Copula hedging:** The only copula-GARCH hedging evidence I retrieved is for crude oil (FI-EGARCH-copula), where a copula plus GARCH-type model gave the best hedging performance. I found no copula study for soybean oil or palm oil. — [Ghorbel et al., FI-EGARCH copula (ResearchGate PDF)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ahmed-Ghorbel-4/publication/280148284_Optimal_hedging_strategy_with_futures_oil_markets_via_FI-EGARCH_copula_model/links/55ecc9d208ae21d099c742a2/Optimal-hedging-strategy-with-futures-oil-markets-via-FI-EGARCH-copula-model.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a soybean-oil importer, a sensible baseline is a rolling-window OLS MVHR on CBOT ZL or DCE Y, matched to the purchase horizon. Adopt DCC/MRS/copula ratios only if they beat this baseline on OOS variance or ES reduction after transaction and rebalancing costs. The literature suggests the gain will be a few percentage points of hedge effectiveness at most.
- The FCPO evidence (HE low, h* about 0.36–0.67) suggests a palm-oil buyer should expect substantial residual risk, partly basis and partly the FCPO-to-physical (CIF/RBD olein) gap. A 1:1 "full hedge" would over-hedge relative to the variance-minimizing ratio.
- ES/CVaR or mean-Gini objectives matter most when the buyer's risk is asymmetric, because input-cost spikes are what hurt. Those objectives tend to produce ratios similar to or slightly above MV, but rigorous OOS ag-specific comparisons were not found.

### Gaps
- No retrieved peer-reviewed 2015–2026 paper gives OOS hedge-effectiveness numbers specifically for CBOT soybean oil with DCC/copula/MRS. The Fan et al. (2016) numbers were not extracted.
- No CVaR/ES-minimizing hedge-ratio study for soybean oil or palm oil with OOS evaluation was found.
- Foundational pointers to check [not verified this session]: Lence (1995), "The economic value of minimum-variance hedges," *AJAE* 77(2), which argued that the welfare gain from sophisticated MV estimation is small. Cotter & Hanly (2006), "Reevaluating hedging performance," *J. Futures Markets* 26(7), on how the choice of HE metric (variance vs VaR/CVaR) changes rankings.

## Q2. Options vs futures for buyers (calls, caps, collars): when do they dominate, and what does premium cost buy?

### Takeaway
Theory says that with unbiased futures and options prices and price-only risk, a risk-minimizing buyer needs only futures, and options add nothing. Options earn a hedging role when there is quantity risk (uncertain volumes or demand) correlated with price, or when the buyer has a view (bias). In practice, calls cap cost but carry a premium, which erodes margins when markets are calm. I found no peer-reviewed 2015–2026 empirical backtest that compares calls or collars with futures for a veg-oil or food-processor buyer.

### Cited Findings
- **Processor model with futures and options under price and basis risk:** When futures and option prices are unbiased, optimal hedging requires only futures. Options matter only if the market is perceived as biased, and then they act as speculative tools. Journal and year were not confirmed from the snippet. — ["Value At Risk: Agricultural Processor Procurement And Hedging Strategies" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23514173_Value_At_Risk_Agricultural_Processor_Procurement_And_Hedging_Strategies)
- **Quantity plus price risk creates a role for options:** Joint production and price risk gives options a hedging role. Simulation supports hedging the minimum expected quantity with futures and the remaining uncertain quantity with options. This is from the producer perspective, but the logic mirrors a buyer's uncertain demand. — ["The Demand for Hedging With Futures and Options" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229542193_The_Demand_for_Hedging_With_Futures_and_Options)
- **Premium mechanics:** A call buyer pays the full premium up front, while a futures hedger posts only a performance bond, which is subject to margin calls when prices fall. The premium is the maximum loss on the call, and it sets a ceiling price of strike + premium + expected basis while keeping the benefit if prices fall. — [CME Group, Self-Study Guide to Hedging with Grain and Oilseed Futures and Options (2019)](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/agricultural/files/pm255_self-study-guide_hedging_en_2019.pdf)
- **Extension view for input buyers:** Calls offer protection against higher prices with limited liability and no margin deposits. Futures alone cannot give upside protection and downside participation together. — [Texas A&M AgriLife, "Risk Management Using Options to Hedge Farm and Ranch Inputs" (E-259)](https://mytexas.ag.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/using-options-to-hedge-farm-and-ranch-inputs.pdf)
- **Layered option programs and premium:** A quarterly layered 12-month program has an average option tenor of about 7.5 months, against 12 months for a rolling 12-month program, which lowers total premium spend. This is a master's thesis, not peer reviewed. — [Jönköping International Business School thesis (DiVA)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:531715/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a soybean-oil importer with firm volumes (contracted sales), futures or forwards dominate on variance per dollar. Calls make sense where (a) import or production volumes are uncertain and positively correlated with price (for example, demand surges in tight markets), (b) margin-call liquidity risk is binding, or (c) the firm cannot pass through cost decreases if it is locked in with futures but competitors are not. That last case is the competitive-exposure argument, which is not verified empirically here.
- Collars (long call, short put) cut the premium but give up downside participation, so economically they sit between futures and calls. No empirical OOS comparison was found.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found that measures realized procurement cost and variance for calls vs collars vs futures for soybean oil, palm oil, or similar buyers (airline fuel-hedging literature may be the nearest analogue; not searched).
- Option implied-vs-realized volatility premia in soybean oil options were not researched. They would determine the expected cost of buying protection.

## Q3. Layered, rolling or DCA hedge programs vs discretionary timing, and does "selective hedging" add value?

### Takeaway
The strongest recent evidence is Fernandez-Perez, Fuertes & Miffre (*Management Science*, 2026). Across 24 commodities, selective hedging driven by futures-return forecasts (from historical means up to ML) generally fails to beat traditional risk-minimizing hedging in expected utility, because futures returns are hard to predict OOS and the speculative component adds risk. This agrees with older firm-level evidence (gold miners, airlines) that selective-hedging gains are small at best. Rigorous academic evidence that specifically compares layered or DCA procurement programs with discretionary timing is sparse. Multi-stage rebalanced hedges beat one-shot MV hedges in simulation.

### Cited Findings
- **Fernandez-Perez, A., Fuertes, A.-M., & Miffre, J. (2026), "Does Speculation in Futures Markets Improve Commodity Hedging Decisions?", *Management Science*, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.04940 (OOS):** The paper covers 24 commodities. Selective hedging overlays forecasts of futures returns on traditional hedging, using historical averages, autoregressive models, forecast combinations, style-integration (carry, momentum and similar factors) and machine learning. Selective hedging "generally fails to outperform traditional hedging in expected utility gains." The authors attribute this to weak OOS predictability and the added risk of the speculative component. Results hold across hedge-ratio specifications, estimation windows, rebalancing frequencies, maturities, risk-aversion levels and sub-periods. The recommendation is that commodity firms prioritize traditional hedging. A practitioner digest of the paper adds that selective hedges did not earn extra return and were "notably worse" in spot-risk coverage. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2024.04940); [City Research Online open-access PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/35792/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-05563835.html); [Bayes Commodity Insights Digest, Summer 2024](https://www.bayes-cid.com/pdf/issues/2024-summer/publications/Commodity_Hedging_Decisions_1709686801.pdf)
- **Adam & Fernando (2006), "Hedging, speculation, and shareholder value," *Journal of Financial Economics* (foundational):** North American gold miners do a lot of selective hedging, but "average cash flow gains from selective hedging are small at best." Derivatives programs overall produced positive cash flows with no offsetting rise in systematic risk. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X05002345)
- **Brown, Crabb & Haushalter (2002/2006), "Are There Speculative Components in Corporate Hedging and Do They Add Value?" (foundational):** In a 48-firm sample (44 gold producers), the potential economic benefit of selective hedging is "quite small." — [DGF 2003 paper PDF](https://www.cofar.uni-mainz.de/dgf2003/paper/paper130.pdf)
- **Airlines, crude oil futures:** Economic gains to selective hedging are small, with no evidence of better operating or financial performance. — ["Can Selective Hedging Add Value to Airlines? The Case of Crude Oil Futures" (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283327201_Can_Selective_Hedging_Add_Value_to_Airlines_The_Case_of_Crude_Oil_Futures)
- **Theory counterpoint:** "How should firms selectively hedge? Resolving the selective hedging puzzle," *Journal of Corporate Finance* (2012), argues that selective hedging can be optimal under some information structures. This is theoretical, not an empirical value finding. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119912000272)
- **Multi-stage rebalanced procurement hedge (EJOR 2012):** A multi-stage strategy that rebalances commodity futures positions for manufacturing-materials procurement "compares favourably" with both the MV hedge and a one-stage hedge in numerical and Monte Carlo experiments. This is simulation, not market OOS. — ["A multi-stage financial hedging approach for the procurement of manufacturing materials," *EJOR* (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221712002408)
- **Practitioner claims (weak, unverified):** A vendor blog says layered structures cut variation in achieved FX rates by up to 75% vs simple rolling programs, citing unnamed back-testing. An uncredited supply-chain page reports procurement cost variance falling from 22% to 7% and suggests hedge ratios of 40–70% of forecast volume. Neither has an identifiable study behind it, so neither should be used as evidence. — [CorpHedge blog](https://blog.corphedge.com/blog/cash-flow-hedging); [supplychainresearch.com](https://www.supplychainresearch.com/research-library/commodity-price-risk-management)
- **Consulting framing:** McKinsey's industrial commodity-risk note frames procurement hedging as a policy choice (coverage ratios, tenor), not market timing. Specific numbers were not extracted. — [McKinsey, "Managing industrials' commodity-price risk"](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/McKinsey/Industries/Electric%20Power%20and%20Natural%20Gas/Our%20Insights/Managing%20industrials%20commodity%20price%20risk/Managing-industrials-commodity-price-risk.pdf)

### Inferences
- The default for a soybean-oil importer should be a rules-based, layered coverage program: for example, rising coverage for nearer months, with the hedge ratio from Q1. Any discretionary or "view" overlay should be small, capped and tracked separately against the rules-based benchmark. The Fernandez-Perez et al. evidence implies the overlay is likely to add risk without reliably adding return.
- Layering mainly smooths purchase cost toward a moving average of forward prices, which cuts timing variance. It does not lower expected cost unless the forward curve is biased. Its value is budget predictability.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that directly measures procurement cost and variance of DCA or layered programs vs discretionary buying for agricultural or veg-oil buyers.
- farmdoc daily and AgMAS (University of Illinois) work on marketing-advisory performance vs benchmarks is producer-side and was not retrieved this session.

## Q4. Real-options and stochastic dynamic programming models for when and how much to buy (inventory + spot + forward + options)

### Takeaway
The OM literature has two mature strands. (1) Structural models solved by SDP show that with forwards available, inventory and financial hedging decisions separate, and price-dependent base-stock policies are optimal. (2) Recent data-driven, "decide-don't-predict" policies (Mandl & Minner; Rettinger, Mandl & Minner) learn parametric purchase rules directly from price and feature data. These report backtested savings of about 4.3% of procurement spend vs a firm's best practice, and up to 6.38% for corn when combining inventory with derivatives. For soybeans and corn, which are storable and seasonal, inventory is the more efficient hedge. For metals, financial instruments are.

### Cited Findings
- **Mandl, C., & Minner, S. (2023), "Data-Driven Optimization for Commodity Procurement Under Price Uncertainty," *M&SOM* 25(2):371–390, DOI 10.1287/msom.2020.0890:** The setting is multiperiod procurement with forward and spot purchases. The method is nonparametric, so no price-process model is needed, and it can use real-time features such as economic indicators. It is formulated as an MILP with ML regularization to limit overfitting and improve OOS generalization. In a 10-year backtest against the company's internal best-practice benchmark, it yields about €9.1M per year, or 4.33% savings. The evaluation is a historical backtest, and the abstract does not fully state the train/test design. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormsom/v25y2023i2p371-390.html); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormsom:v:25:y:2023:i:2:p:371-390); [Supplement (INFORMS)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/msom.2020.0890/suppl_file/msom.2020.0890.sm1.pdf)
- **Rettinger, M., Mandl, C., & Minner, S. (2024), "A data-driven approach for optimal operational and financial commodity hedging," *EJOR* 316(1):341–360, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2024.01.026 (open access, CC BY):** The authors prove optimality of a policy that combines inventory (operational) and derivatives (financial), with decision granularity independent of contract maturity, trained by MIP. In a 10-year backtest on copper, nickel, corn and soybean, the combined policy gives average savings of up to 6.38% for corn vs single-instrument strategies, because warehousing offsets seasonality. Corn and soybean are hedged more efficiently via inventory, and copper and nickel via financial instruments. Fewer-parameter (coarser) decision granularity generalizes best OOS, because high-frequency decisions hurt learning stability. — [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:316:y:2024:i:1:p:341-360); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221724000456)
- **Kouvelis, P., Pang, Z., & Ding, Q. (2018), "Integrated Commodity Inventory Management and Financial Hedging: A Dynamic Mean-Variance Analysis," *Production and Operations Management*, DOI 10.1111/poms.12853:** This is a dynamic mean-variance SDP. With forward contracts, a myopic state-dependent base-stock policy is optimal, and inventory and financial hedging decisions can be separated. This is a theoretical result. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/poms.12853)
- **"Dynamic Risk Management of Commodity Operations: Model and Analysis," *M&SOM*, DOI 10.1287/msom.2017.0647:** A dynamic risk-management model for commodity operations. Authors and findings were not extracted, but the reference is verified. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/msom.2017.0647)
- **"Risk management through financial hedging in inventory systems with stochastic price processes," *IJPE* (2024):** Characterizes the per-period minimum-variance financial hedge by solving a stochastic DP alongside inventory decisions. — [ScienceDirect, IJPE S092552732400046X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092552732400046X)
- **"Financial Hedging and Optimal Procurement Policies under Correlated Price and Demand" (Bilkent):** Models combined sourcing (spot + financial hedges) with correlated price and demand as a stochastic dynamic program. — [Bilkent PDF](https://yoksis.bilkent.edu.tr/pdf/files/13166.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317177998_Financial_Hedging_and_Optimal_Procurement_Policies_under_Correlated_Price_and_Demand)
- **"Orders and inventory commodities with price and demand uncertainty in complete markets," *IJPE* (2008, foundational):** Derives the optimal combined purchasing policy by SDP. Shows that a zero-inventory policy can be replicated by a portfolio of options plus an optimal order quantity, which is the real-options link. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925527308001230)
- **"Optimal Purchasing Policy For Mean-Reverting Items in a Finite Horizon" (arXiv 1711.03188):** Gives purchase-timing rules under mean-reverting prices. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03188)

### Inferences
- For a soybean-oil buyer the practical recipe is a parametric, price-dependent purchase rule. For example, cover x% forward plus inventory build when the price is below a threshold relative to the forward curve or features. Train it Mandl–Minner-style on historical data with coarse decision granularity and regularization, and evaluate it on a rolling OOS backtest vs the firm's current practice. A 2–6% spend saving is the empirically reported range for corn and soybeans and for an industrial benchmark, but those are not veg-oil numbers.
- The separation result (Kouvelis et al.) supports organizing the work in two pieces. Size inventory by base-stock logic, then hedge the residual price exposure with futures or forwards.
- Soybean oil has finite shelf life and storage costs, so the "inventory as hedge" advantage found for corn and soybeans may be weaker for oil than for beans.

### Gaps
- No retrieved paper applies these data-driven or SDP procurement models specifically to soybean oil or palm oil.
- Pointers to check [not verified this session]: Goel & Gutierrez (2011), "Multiechelon procurement and distribution policies for traded commodities," *Management Science* 57(12):2228–2244 (a search result cited a *Management Science* 57(12):2228–2244 entry without its title). Berling & Martínez-de-Albéniz (2011), "Optimal inventory policies when purchase price and demand are stochastic," *Operations Research* 59(1). Secomandi & Kekre (2014), "Optimal energy procurement in spot and forward markets," *M&SOM*.

## Q5. Basis risk for importers hedging with CBOT futures (cross-hedging, origin/CIF basis)

### Takeaway
For an importer, especially in Asia, the hedge has three legs: CBOT futures, origin FOB premium plus freight (the CIF basis), and FX. The basis legs are often the least hedgeable part. Chinese crushers hedge beans on CBOT and sell DCE meal and oil, but report being unable to hedge volatile basis. CBOT-to-DCE risk spillovers are strong, but DCE reflects domestic conditions. Alternative contracts (USD-settled Bursa soybean oil) claim closer tracking of Asian physical flows than CME ZL. No peer-reviewed study found quantifies hedge effectiveness of a CBOT soybean-oil hedge against CIF Asian physical prices.

### Cited Findings
- **Industry structure:** Chinese crushers importing US beans typically hedge on CBOT and sell DCE meal and oil futures. DCE reflects Chinese domestic conditions, while CBOT prices global supply and trade. — [Stroud, US-China Economic and Security Review Commission testimony, April 16, 2026](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/Susan_Stroud_Testimony.pdf)
- **Basis risk realized:** In 2023 Chinese crushers "were unable to hedge the basis risk" amid volatile basis movements (trade press). — [S&P Global Commodity Insights, Mar 2023](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/031723-china-soybean-prices-plunge-amid-weaker-local-demand-higher-brazil-farmer-sales)
- **Spillovers:** Extreme risk spills over from US (CBOT) soybean futures to China's soybean-linked futures, including soybean oil. — [Qin et al. (2024), "Extreme Risk Spillovers From US Soybean Futures Market to China's Soybean-Linked Futures Markets," *Journal of Futures Markets*, DOI 10.1002/fut.22542](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fut.22542). A related study finds CBOT soybeans have the largest spillover to DCE No.1 soybeans, with volatility spillover from the US to China weakening in recent years. — [arXiv 2403.01745](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01745v1)
- **USDA report effects on DCE:** Information transmits from USDA reports to Chinese soybean-complex futures. — [Hu & Mallory, NCCC-134 2022](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_2022/pdf/Hu_Mallory_NCCC-134_2022.pdf)
- **Trade-war rewiring:** The Sino-US trade war changed time-varying and frequency-domain soybean price linkages, which implies unstable cross-market hedge ratios. — [*Foods* (MDPI) 15(10):1678, DOI 10.3390/foods15101678](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods15101678)
- **Instrument choice:** Bursa Malaysia's marketing report argues that its USD-settled soybean oil contract tracks Asian or Chinese physical prices more closely, and that CME soybean oil is lower in correlation to physical soybean oil flows. This is an exchange marketing claim, not independent evidence. — [Bursa Malaysia Soybean Focus Report, Apr 2024](https://www.bursamalaysia.com/sites/5d809dcf39fba22790cad230/assets/6628a707e6414ab802ac7751/Soybean_Focus_Report_BURSA_APRIL_2024-final.pdf)
- **Palm-oil cross-hedging:** For physical CPO, the best non-palm cross-hedge among energy futures was Tokyo crude oil. FCPO's own hedge effectiveness is low (see Q1), which is consistent with large basis between futures and physical. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333842962_Effective_cross_hedging_Evidence_from_physical_crude_palm_oil_and_its_non-interrelated_energy_futures_contracts); [Ong & Tan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267781069_Hedging_Effectiveness_of_Crude_Palm_Oil_Futures_Market_in_Malaysia)
- **Cross-hedging basics for ag:** The cross-hedge ratio is estimated by regressing local cash price changes on futures changes. Hedge effectiveness is bounded by the R². — [Kansas State University, "Cross Hedging Agricultural Commodities"](https://www.coffey.k-state.edu/farm-management/Cross%20Hedging%20Agricultural%20Commoditie.pdf); [Alabama Extension](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/farm-management/marketing-tools-cross-hedging/)

### Inferences
- A Korean or Asian soybean-oil importer hedging with CBOT ZL should (a) estimate the hedge ratio on its own landed-cost series (CIF + FX), not on ZL alone; (b) treat the FOB premium (Brazil/Argentina/US Gulf) and freight as separate risk factors, hedged through fixed-premium purchase contracts or freight FFAs where possible; and (c) test DCE Y, Bursa FCPO or Bursa USD soybean oil as candidate hedges or composites by OOS variance reduction. Palm and soy oil substitution and correlation may make a two-instrument hedge better than ZL alone.
- Because cross-market linkages shift with trade policy (the trade-war evidence), the hedge ratio should be re-estimated on a rolling window. Regime-aware models may help more for cross-hedges than for direct hedges, though this is unverified.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate found of minimum-variance hedge ratio or hedge effectiveness for CBOT soybean oil against CIF Korea, CIF China, or other Asian physical soybean-oil prices.
- No quantitative study found of FOB-premium (paranaguá/Rosario/Gulf) basis volatility as a share of total landed-cost variance for veg oils.
- farmdoc daily analyses of soybean oil basis were not retrieved this session.
